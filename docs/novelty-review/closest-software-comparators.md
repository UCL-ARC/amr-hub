# Closest software comparators for AMR-Hub

Recorded 29 October 2026 following the investigator's request. This supplements the [publication review](../amr-hub-novelty-and-publication-review.md).

The comparison concerns the planned year-long UCLH ward study: routine staff location/activity records, CAD geometry, patient-linked positive and negative clinical tests, and patient stays inferred from testing timelines. Clinical data remain restricted. Similarity rankings are editorial assessments of documented capabilities, not benchmark results.

For AMR-Hub, staff records and CAD are planned clinical inputs; patient-linked positive/negative clinical tests support observation modelling, and stays are inferred. Current CPU outputs are movement/contact measurements. Infection inference remains a proposed extension. Open clinical data are not required for software to be open source; code licensing and data access are separate questions.

See also the [five-criteria comparison](five-criteria-comparison.md), including verification of AMR-Hub's BDI and organism/strain scope.

## Recommended inspection order

Examine **RTLS_ABM, AMRinference and H-Outbreak** first. Together they cover movement-driven simulation, inference under incomplete clinical observation and an inspectable Python hospital simulator. H-Outbreak was identified in the follow-up software search and deserves inclusion in the eventual manuscript literature review; it was omitted from the initial 36-source draft.

The potential AMR-Hub distinction is the combination of staff-resolved workflow with explicit uncertainty in patient presence and clinical observation. None of the inspected descriptions establishes that exact combination. This is a candidate distinction requiring further investigation, not a priority or “first” claim.

## RTLS_ABM: follow-up details

- **Terminology:** RTLS means Real-Time Location System; ABM means agent-based model. This study uses ultra-wideband (UWB) tracking.
- **Sampling:** Section 4.4 reports locations at one-second intervals (1 Hz), used to update simulated staff/device positions. This is the study's reported interval, not a universal RTLS specification or assurance of complete coverage.
- **Geometry:** the hospital floorplan was digitised and integrated into AnyLogic. The monitored fifth-floor area comprised three main hallways, three nurse stations and 22 inpatient rooms; Appendix A, Figure 8 shows the layout. Original CAD versus image/PDF format and an automated topology-extraction pipeline have not been verified.
- **Coverage limitation:** corridor-mounted anchors did not provide complete coverage inside patient rooms. Room visits were inferred from proximity to access points. The earlier shorthand “measured trajectories” must not imply continuously observed movement everywhere.
- **Site:** the paper describes a post-surgery observation unit in a tertiary hospital in the authors' city. Author affiliations place the work in Kansas City, Missouri, and include University Health Kansas City. The specific hospital/campus has not been explicitly verified; an affiliation is not sufficient evidence to identify the deployment site.

Sources: [paper, especially Sections 3, 4.1, 4.4 and Appendix A](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12990909/), [CDC-hosted manuscript](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/260733/cdc_260733_DS1.pdf), [PubMed abstract and affiliations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41847591/). Details recorded following the investigator's questions; no hospital identity inferred beyond the evidence above.

## AMRinference: staff movement distinction

The published AMRinference model does not explicitly use observed healthcare-worker movement. Its contact network is constructed from patient hospitalization/location records. The authors identify the absence of explicit staff contacts as a limitation and propose a bipartite healthcare-worker/patient network if suitable data become available. This does not mean staff-mediated transmission is biologically absent; it means observed staff trajectories are not represented explicitly in that implementation.

For the planned UCLH study, AMR-Hub has routine staff location/activity information but lacks direct patient movement and admission/discharge records. AMRinference has stronger patient-presence information. These are complementary observational strengths, not evidence that either model is universally more detailed or valid. Source: [Pei et al. (2025), Discussion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65241-w).

## H-Outbreak: staff representation and temporal resolution

The paper explicitly states that patients are the only agents. It does not use observed staff movement records or represent individual healthcare workers as agents. References to staff shifts and cleaning policies concern temporal constraints and environmental rules, not measured staff trajectories. The published example uses eight-hour simulation steps and represents transmission between patients and through contaminated locations.

The simulated hospital structure is based on Rafael Méndez General University Hospital in Murcia, Spain. This structural basis does not establish ingestion of CAD geometry or reconstruction of a real patient/staff cohort. Source: [Kim et al. (2023), Methods: Microscale simulation and Simulating Clostridioides difficile infection in a midsize hospital in Spain](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-47296-1).

## Verification limits

Publisher/project pages and public repository descriptions were checked. The RTLS_ABM repository explicitly links its model to the Thota publication; H-Outbreak's repository links its Scientific Reports paper; the AMRinference paper identifies its public code and example data. These packages were not installed, executed or benchmarked. No claim is made about production readiness, maintenance quality, equivalent scientific mechanisms or suitability as a drop-in replacement. Licence findings were subsequently checked as recorded in the availability table above; component-specific reuse terms still need review before incorporating code.
